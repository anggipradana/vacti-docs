Vulnerability Assessment (VA) is the product you use to scan your own domains for weaknesses. You add a target, run a recon-driven scan, and get back a list of findings grouped by severity, a risk picture for the workspace, and reports you can share. VA is the starting point for most work in Vacti: its results also show up in the Attack Surface and Cyber Threat Intelligence pages, and they feed the flagship [AI Pentest](/products/00-ai-pentest/) product, which orchestrates a full engagement on top of VA, Attack Surface, CTI, and Leaks.

Open it from the top navigation as **Vulnerability Assessment** (route `/scans`). The page description reads "Recon runs across your targets."

## Who can use it

- Anyone signed in to a workspace can open VA and read its scans and findings.
- Starting a scan needs the initiate-scans permission. SuperAdmin and PenetrationTester have it; Auditor does not.
- Changing a finding's status, note, or details needs the scan-results-modify permission. Auditors see findings read-only.
- Adding or editing targets needs the targets-modify permission.

Everything is scoped to the workspace you pick in the header switcher, so you only ever see the targets and results that belong to the selected workspace.

## What you see at a glance

The top of the page is a posture strip with four tiles:

- **Running now**: how many scans are in progress, with a live indicator when something is active.
- **Last scan**: the status of the most recent scan and how long ago it ran.
- **Open findings**: the total number of open findings across the workspace, broken down by severity with a colored dot per level.
- **Targets**: how many targets the workspace has.

When a scan is queued or running, a **Running now** band appears below the strip. Each active scan is a card showing the target, a status pill, a stage stepper, and a **Cancel** button. The page refreshes on its own while anything is running and stops polling once everything is finished.

## Add a target

Targets are managed in the **Targets** panel at the bottom of the page. It opens automatically when a workspace has no targets yet. For each target you set:

- **Domain**: the host to assess, for example `example.com`.
- **Predefined subdomains**: optional, space separated. Leave empty to let Vacti discover subdomains for you.
- **Custom request headers**: optional, one per line, for example an `Authorization` or API-key header so the scan can reach an authenticated surface.

Each target row shows the domain, a badge when custom headers are set, and either a count of predefined subs or a discovery-on badge. You can edit a target, open per-target notes, or delete it along with all its scans.

## Run a scan

Click **New scan** in the header and set:

- **Target**: one of the workspace's targets.
- **Mode**:
  - **Active**: runs the recon pipeline against the target.
  - **Passive**: pulls OSINT only, with no traffic to the target. Passive results live on the Attack Surface page.
  - **Full**: passive discovery first, then the active pipeline.
- **Timeout**: how long each tool may run. The default is 60 minutes, up to 600 minutes for large multi-domain scans.
- **Deep-fetch discovered URLs**: an optional checkbox for passive or full scans that fetches response bodies to look for exposed secrets.
- **Scan profile**: shown only when saved profiles exist. Leave as the default to run all stages, or pick a profile.
- **Run on engine**: an optional preference, shown when scan engines are available.

Click **Start scan**. Vacti creates the scan and takes you to its detail page.

The [Run a scan](/user/03-run-a-scan/) walkthrough covers these steps in more detail.

## The scan detail page

Open any scan to see its full results (route `/scans/[id]`).

- The header shows the target and a status pill. While a scan is unfinished you can **Cancel** it; once finished you can **Delete** it.
- For an active or full scan the header offers **Attack Path** and **Generate report**. For a passive scan it offers **View on Attack Surface**.
- A progress card shows the stage stepper and an activity timeline.
- Results are grouped into tabs with counts: **Endpoints**, **Ports**, **Subdomains**, and **Vulnerabilities**.

The Endpoints tab lists discovered URLs with their status code, page title, and detected technologies, flagging interesting or WordPress endpoints. The Ports and Subdomains tabs list what was found. The Vulnerabilities tab is where findings live.

## Where findings land

Findings appear on the **Vulnerabilities** tab of the scan that produced them. Vacti does not show one global findings list; you open a scan to see its findings. The tab shows a severity donut above the findings table.

Each finding row carries a severity badge, the finding name, and its location. Expand a row to read the template description, remediation, CVSS score, CVE ids, and references, any AI-generated analysis, and the raw request and response.

With the scan-results-modify permission you can:

- Change a finding's status with the per-row selector.
- Add or edit an analyst note.
- Select rows and apply a status in bulk, run AI enrich to generate description, impact, and remediation, or clear the selection.
- Delete a finding.

Findings are paged 50 at a time. See [Findings](/user/04-findings/) for the full triage flow.

## Risk score and acceptance

The workspace risk posture is summarized two ways:

- The **Open findings** tile on the VA page counts open findings by severity for the whole workspace.
- The **Unified risk score** on the Cyber Threat Intelligence page brings VA findings together with leaked credentials, exposure findings, and threat intelligence into a single 0 to 100 score. The score is color banded: green for low, yellow for the middle range, and red for high. Triaging a finding (for example marking it a false positive or remediated) lowers the score, so keeping findings reviewed keeps the number honest.

There is no separate sign-off step for a VA finding: its status (for example open, false positive, or remediated) is the record of your decision, and that status is reflected in both the open-findings count and the risk score.

## Compare, rescan, and attack path

When a scan has finished the detail page offers a compare-and-rescan card:

- **Compare with an earlier scan**: pick an earlier completed scan of the same target to see added, removed, and unchanged subdomains, endpoints, ports, and vulnerabilities.
- **Rescan**: re-run the scan. For an active scan you can uncheck individual tools to run a partial sub-scan; for a passive scan it re-runs OSINT only.

For an active or full scan, **Attack Path** lays your in-scope findings out as a kill chain, grouping findings by host across phases from recon through to impact, with a host-by-severity heatmap. Findings marked as false positives are excluded.

## Reports

From a finished active or full scan you can open a VA report in a new tab in three forms: a full report, a recon-only report, and a findings-only report. See [Reports](/user/06-reports/) for what each one contains.
